## Controller: The Native Tongue

The straightforward half first: plug in a controller and Bloodborne PC speaks its native tongue. Xbox-layout pads pair seamlessly through Windows, PlayStation-style pads work through the OS layer with the usual minor ceremony, and the combat reads exactly as muscle memory expects — the original's control scheme translated one-to-one, because the engine executing is the original's engine. The recommendation from the port's first weeks is unambiguous: if you own a controller, use it for your first Bloodborne PC session — the game's camera-and-dodge grammar was composed for sticks and triggers, and the port's fidelity to the original feel is one of its most consistent compliments. Controller-first is not a compromise; it is the game as it was designed, at frame rates the designer's hardware never allowed.

## Mouse: The Experiment That Works

The port's genuinely novel offering: mouse controls for a Soulslike. The mouse's natural strengths — camera authority and aim precision — map well onto Bloodborne PC's most PC-alien demands: the free-look camera that console players fought with the right stick, and the ranged tools that benefit from direct pointing. The challenges are equally structural: dodge timing and the multi-button combat grammar live naturally under thumbs, not on a keyboard's left bank, and the mouse learning curve for a game this fast is measured in hours, not minutes. The honest guidance: mouse play is not a gimmick — the port implements it well enough that some first-week players have adopted it as their preferred way to play Bloodborne PC — but it is an adaptation, a second accent for a game composed in a first. Try it; keep the controller within reach; let your hands vote.

## Choosing Your Input, Decided

Because the three-input situation leaves some hunters paralyzed at the character creator, the decision tree that resolves it cleanly. **You own a controller and have played any Souls game**: use the controller — your muscle memory is an asset worth an entire difficulty tier, and the port reproduces the native feel faithfully; there is nothing to gain from relearning the combat's physical grammar on new hardware. **You own a controller but have never played a Soulslike**: still start with the controller — the genre's combat was composed for it — but give the mouse an honest hour once the basics land, because Bloodborne PC's mouse implementation is good enough that some lifers have switched, and the camera authority genuinely transforms the learning experience for players weaned on PC shooters. **You own no controller**: mouse-and-keyboard without hesitation — the port's implementation is complete and playable, the dodge-on-keyboard learning curve is real but a few hours shallow, and waiting to buy hardware before entering Yharnam is a form of procrastination the blood moon does not reward. The meta-truth across all three branches: Bloodborne PC's port team built three complete input paths because they wanted no hunter turned away at the door — the only wrong choice is agonizing over the choice while Central Yharnam waits unexplored. Pick the input your hands already speak, sharpen the settings to your display, and go — the game will teach you the rest, frame by unlocked frame.
