Bloodborne on PC, twice over: the bbport runs the game natively on Windows, and shadPS4 — the open-source PlayStation 4 emulator — runs it through software emulation, mature and complete with players finishing full playthroughs at 4K 60 FPS. Two roads to Yharnam, each with real strengths, and this page compares them honestly across the axes that decide: performance, compatibility and completeness, hardware demands, features, and the legal-situational realities both share. It concludes, because comparisons should.

## The Two Architectures

The fundamental difference first, because everything downstream flows from it. The bbport is a native port — the game's engine compiled for Windows, executing directly on your hardware with the port's own modifications, settings and online infrastructure layered on top. shadPS4 is an emulator — software that reconstitutes a PlayStation 4 in your machine and runs the original, unmodified game inside it, translating console instructions on the fly. The native port's advantages are efficiency (no emulation overhead), modification freedom (the QoL improvements, the online play, the mouse controls exist because the port can change the game) and performance accessibility (60 FPS on low-end hardware). The emulator's advantages are fidelity and completeness — it runs the actual PS4 build, unmodified, and its compatibility is the whole game rather than the port's deepest-traveled sections. Neither approach is better; they optimize for different definitions of the goal.

## Performance and Hardware

The bbport's performance story: 60 FPS reportedly on low-end hardware, 100 FPS at the RX 6650 XT mid-range, scaling upward — efficiency is the native port's structural gift, and Bloodborne PC via bbport is accessible to nearly any modern machine. The shadPS4 story: the emulator is mature — Digital Foundry-tier coverage has documented 4K 60 FPS playthroughs on strong hardware, and the compatibility is complete enough for full runs — but the emulation overhead means the hardware floor is higher: strong CPUs and GPUs carry the translation layer's cost, and the low-end machines that run the bbport happily are not the machines that emulate PS4s. The performance verdict by tier: low-end hardware → bbport, clearly; strong hardware → both perform excellently, and the choice moves to the other axes.

## Completeness and Features

The honest differentiator: shadPS4 plays the entire game, start to finish, at full compatibility — the emulator's completeness is its defining strength, and hunters who want the guaranteed-whole experience have their answer. The bbport is deepest in the early and mid game with later sections still rough, improving on the update cycle — but it carries features the emulator cannot offer: the frame-rate unlock, the QoL suite, the mouse controls, and above all the working online play with its matchmaking infrastructure, which emulation's Sony-server-shaped hole has never filled. The feature verdict: completeness-critical players → shadPS4; the online multiplayer and the PC-native feature set → bbport, full stop — no emulator route delivers what the port's matchmaking delivers.

## Which Road for Which Hunter

By player, because that is how this decides. **The first-time player** who wants the guaranteed complete journey: shadPS4 — the whole game, no rough edges in the map's far corners, at emulator-appropriate hardware. **The returning veteran** who wants the game again at its best feel: bbport — 100 FPS, the QoL suite, and the online bells ringing again are the reunion the veteran is buying. **The low-end hardware player**: bbport — the port's efficiency floor is dramatically lower than emulation's. **The online multiplayer hunter**: bbport — the matchmaking website is the only path to Bloodborne's multiplayer on PC, period. **The preservation-minded**: both — the emulator preserves the artifact, the port reimagines it, and Bloodborne's PC afterlife is healthier with both alive. The meta-verdict, same as this wiki's other comparisons: the two roads are complements wearing rivals' clothes — and the extraordinary fact of October 2026 is not which one wins but that both exist, eleven years after anyone official stopped pretending a PC version might come.
