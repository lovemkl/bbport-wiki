Questions about Bloodborne PC cluster into the same handful across forums, store pages and search boxes. This page answers them straight — facts dated, gray labeled gray, and pointers into the wiki's deeper pages. It updates as the port's situation does.

## Is There an Official Bloodborne PC Version?

No. Sony has never released Bloodborne on PC — no port, no remaster, no remake — and former Sony executive Shuhei Yoshida, himself a fan, called the lack of a version a mystery even to him. The bbport is an unofficial community project, not licensed by Sony or FromSoftware, existing in the long-tolerated gray zone of preservation-oriented fan work. Every page on this wiki labels it accordingly.

## What Is the bbport?

An unofficial native Windows port of Bloodborne, released October 2026 — roughly eleven years after the PS4 original. It runs the game's engine natively on PC with real graphics settings, QoL improvements, frame rates up to 100 FPS and beyond on mid-range hardware, working online play with its own matchmaking website, and mouse controls. It reportedly runs well even on low-end machines at 60 FPS, and there's a Linux port too. The complete guide covers the whole story.

## Is the Port Complete?

Mostly — it runs the game start to finish, but coverage notes it isn't complete end-to-end: later sections may still have rough edges, with the early and mid game the most polished. The port improves on the community's update cycle. For the guaranteed complete experience, the shadPS4 emulator route plays the entire game at full compatibility — the comparison page helps you choose.

## Does Online Play Really Work?

Yes — the port's boldest feature. Working online functionality with its own matchmaking website restores co-op summoning and invasion play, the multiplayer that defined the original's community. The online guide covers the matchmaking setup, the current state, and the etiquette forming around the second-life community.

## How Well Does It Run?

Up to 100 FPS on an RX 6650 XT per launch-window testing, reportedly solid 60 FPS on low-end hardware, and scaling upward with stronger machines. The performance page maps hardware tiers to expected frame rates and walks the settings that matter. The floor is roughly double the original's locked 30 — nearly any modern PC clears it.

## Controller or Mouse?

Both work. Controllers deliver the game as designed — the combat was born on sticks, and the port reproduces the original feel natively. Mouse controls are the port's novel experiment — genuinely playable, with real strengths in camera control, and a learning curve measured in hours. The controls guide covers the full landscape; the short advice is controller-first, try the mouse if curious.
