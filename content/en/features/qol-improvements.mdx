## Real Settings, Finally

The original Bloodborne had no settings — one resolution, one frame rate, one look, take it or leave it on a living room TV. The bbport's settings suite is the most fundamental QoL improvement because it enables all the others: resolution and display options that respect your monitor, graphics settings that let you tune the experience to your machine and taste, frame-behavior controls that put the cadence in your hands, and audio options that route the game's excellent mix properly on PC hardware. The settings suite is also the port's philosophy made visible — every option is something the original denied, shipped by people who spent eleven years on the wrong side of the denial.

## Display and Readability

The display QoL extends past resolution: the port supports the aspect ratios and multi-monitor arrangements PC players expect, and the readability improvements — text and UI scaling options the original lacked — matter more than they sound for a game whose lore lives in item descriptions and whose interface was authored for couch distance. Players on high-DPI displays get sharpness the console never delivered; players who found the original's text small get scaling that fixes it. These are small dignities individually and a genuinely modernized experience collectively — the difference between a port that runs on PC and a port that lives there.

## Input QoL

The controls guide covers the input landscape in depth; the QoL layer on top includes the remapping support, the mouse implementation itself (a QoL feature from the perspective of players whose hands live on mouse and keyboard), and the responsiveness tuning that the port's native execution delivers as a side effect of being native — input latency at PC-native levels rather than console-pipeline levels, which in a dodge-timing game is a quality-of-life feature disguised as an engineering detail.
